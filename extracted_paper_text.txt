Abstract—Enterprise operational governance fundamentally
depends upon two distinct, heterogeneous knowledge spaces:
unstructured Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) text man-
uals and structured Business Process Model and Notation
(BPMN) workflow models. While Large Language Models
(LLMs) combined with conventional Retrieval-Augmented
Generation (RAG) demonstrate strong performance in open-
domain factual synthesis, they exhibit critical inductive limi-
tations when deployed in procedural enterprise environments.
Specifically, standard dense vector retrieval suffers from topo-
logical scrambling of sequential execution milestones due to
metric distance symmetry, as well as severe branch ambiguity
at conditional decision gateways induced by semantic proximity
between opposing operational branches. Conversely, existing
GraphRAG architectures capture static relational entity triples
(h, r, t) but remain oblivious to dynamic execution semantics,
transition states, and organizational swimlanes.
To resolve this dichotomy, this paper addresses enterprise
process question answering as an adaptive routing and reason-
ing problem over heterogeneous knowledge spaces. We propose
an Adaptive Agentic Hybrid RAG framework with BPMN-
grounded graph reasoning. The architecture coordinates four
specialized modules: (1) a Query Understanding Agent that
performs domain-specific acronym expansion and canonical
entity grounding; (2) an Adaptive Router Agent that evaluates
query intent to allocate dynamic retrieval quotas across vector
stores and directed process graphs; (3) a Cross-Modal Con-
text Fusion Layer that projects unstructured text onto graph
topology while enforcing strict institutional domain boundaries;
and (4) an Adversarial Critic Verification Agent that enforces
factual entailment, workflow sequence precedence, swimlane
role attribution, and checklist completeness through closed-
loop iterative self-repair.
We benchmark the framework on the ICICoS ProcessQA
dataset suite ( N = 338 expert-curated questions across four
operational categories). In empirical benchmark evaluation on a
stratified test sample, the proposed framework achieves a Faith-
fulness score of 0.972 and suppresses the Hallucination Rate
to 2.6% (representing a 76.4% relative error reduction versus
Traditional Vector RAG and 79.5% versus Static Hybrid RAG),
alongside a Router Dispatch Accuracy of 65.0% (lenient match;
74.9% strict full corpus) and Actor Attribution Accuracy of
93.8% while achieving 1.000 Context Recall on the evaluated
benchmark sample at an end-to-end operational latency of
18.9s (reflecting closed-loop adversarial verification). Rigorous
non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank tests ( W = 6 .0, p =
0.000219) and paired t-tests ( t = 5 .619, p = 0 .000020) con-
firm statistically significant improvements over Hybrid RAG,
while 10,000-resample Bootstrap 95% Confidence Intervals
([0.9507, 0.9910]) demonstrate non-overlapping separation over
all baselines. These findings establish that deterministic process
graph grounding combined with adaptive agentic orchestration
provides a viable foundation for audit-compliant enterprise
process intelligence.

I. Introduction
The integration of conversational artificial intelligence
into enterprise operations represents a transformative shift
in organizational decision support systems, regulatory
governance, and business process automation [1], [2]. In
contemporary administrative and industrial ecosystems,
operational integrity is governed by strict adherence to
Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), regulatory statu-
tory mandates, and institutional service level agreements
(SLAs). While enterprise process intelligence spans di-
verse corporate domains, this study utilizes the complete,
auditable operational ecosystem of academic conference
administration (ICICoS) as a representative empirical
testbed. Academic conference lifecycle management pos-
sesses the formal dual-modal characteristics of large-
scale enterprise governance: legal author guidelines and
financial honoraria rules (declarative text) paired with
rigorous, multi-role review and publication workflows
(ISO/IEC 19510 BPMN models). Foundation models can

significantly augment administrative productivity across
document summarization, policy drafting, and conversa-
tional inquiry resolution [2]. However, the deployment of
general conversational agents in enterprise environments
remains contingent on their capability to interface reliably
with private, highly specialized operational guidelines
that are absent from public pretraining corpora. Conse-
quently, organizational deployment demands specialized
architectures capable of grounding generative synthesis in
authoritative internal knowledge repositories.
Standalone autoregressive language models are funda-
mentally incapable of serving as autonomous enterprise
assistants due to their exclusive reliance on static para-
metric memory [3], [4]. Empirical investigations reveal
that foundation models exhibit systemic hallucinations,
confabulating non-existent policies, conflicting regulatory
requirements, and fabricated compliance deadlines [3].
Furthermore, because these models operate as black-box
predictors without native provenance mechanisms, they
cannot provide verifiable audit trails or enforce verifiable
compliance constraints across institutional swimlanes and
role authorizations. In mission-critical workflows such as
conference budget disbursement, academic accreditation,
or industrial compliance, an ungrounded hallucination rep-
resents an existential compliance risk. This vulnerability
underscores the imperative for non-parametric grounding
mechanisms that enforce factual provenance and prevent
regulatory confabulation.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged
as the standard paradigm to decouple parametric genera-
tion from external factual knowledge [5], [6]. Groundbreak-
ing frameworks by Lewis et al. [5] and dense dual-encoder
architectures such as DPR [7] and Sentence-BERT [8] have
substantially improved question answering by retrieving
semantically relevant text passages using vector similarity.
Nonetheless, the dominant dense retrieval paradigm relies
on an unconstrained metric space where similarity is
fundamentally symmetric (dist (u,v ) = dist(v,u )), ren-
dering vector search topologically blind to chronological
precedence and sequential ordering [9], [10]. In procedural
enterprise environments, this geometric symmetry induces
topological scrambling, where prerequisite conditions and
subsequent actions are retrieved in arbitrary configura-
tions.
Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation
(GraphRAG) has recently been introduced to mitigate
text chunk fragmentation by capturing structural
relational associations [11]–[13]. Recent works by Edge et
al. [11], He et al. [12], and knowledge graph reasoning
frameworks [14]–[16] demonstrate that constructing
relational entity graphs enhances multi-hop reasoning
and broad contextual summarization. However, existing
GraphRAG methodologies model static entity-relationship
triples (h,r,t ) rather than formal business execution
graphs. They lack dynamic execution semantics, event
triggers, lifecycle states, and exclusive/parallel decision
gateways codified in standard process notations such
as BPMN 2.0 [17]. Consequently, relational knowledge
graphs cannot differentiate between mutually exclusive
alternative branches and parallel execution paths in
organizational workflows.
Enterprise process question answering fundamentally
differs from standard open-domain retrieval because en-
terprise knowledge is bifurcated into two mutually exclu-
sive epistemic structures: descriptive textual knowledge
and sequential process topology [18], [19]. Organizational
operational manuals, checklists, and fee schedules repre-
sent unstructured declarative knowledge, whereas busi-
ness workflows formalize temporal dependencies, condi-
tional branching, and swimlane role allocations [20]. A
purely document-centric inquiry (e.g., “What mandatory
attachments must accompany an honorarium payment
voucher?”) can be resolved through semantic passage
retrieval; conversely, a procedural workflow inquiry (e.g.,
“What sequence of actions occurs after a payment invoice
is rejected by faculty finance?”) cannot be answered by
vector proximity alone, as opposing conditional branches
(approval vs. rejection) occupy near-identical embedding
coordinates. This divergence establishes that neither text
retrieval nor process modeling in isolation can satisfy
enterprise operational inquiries.
Agentic RAG architectures introduce autonomous plan-
ning and iterative self-reflection to dynamically optimize
retrieval-generation cycles [21]–[24]. Prominent frame-
works such as Self-RAG [21], Adaptive-RAG [22], and
CRAG [23] incorporate reflection tokens and dynamic tool
dispatch to evaluate retrieval necessity and trigger exter-
nal web searches. Nevertheless, current agentic systems
remain confined to selecting external search tools over
homogeneous textual collections; they possess no mech-
anism to arbitrate between fundamentally heterogeneous
knowledge representations (such as unstructured narrative
text manuals versus structured directed process graphs).
Static concatenation of text and graph data in these
frameworks induces severe context pollution and cross-
modal context starvation.
Synthesizing these theoretical limitations reveals three
unresolved research gaps that constrain enterprise process
intelligence:
1) Research Gap 1 (Absence of Workflow Topology
in Vector RAG): Dense embeddings map text into
an unordered metric space where vector distance

induces branch ambiguity at conditional gateways.
2) Research Gap 2 (Lack of Process Execution Seman-
tics in GraphRAG): Existing graph RAG models
static entity associations while remaining oblivious
to BPMN execution flow, conditional decision gates,
and swimlane accountabilities.
3) Research Gap 3 (Lack of Heterogeneous Represen-
tation Routing in Agentic RAG): Current agentic
frameworks lack adaptive routing mechanisms capa-

II. Related Work
A. Large Language Models for Enterprise Knowledge
Management
Autoregressive language models pretrained on web-scale
corpora have demonstrated exceptional zero-shot synthesis
capabilities across corporate domains [1]. However, foun-
dation models rely on static parametric memory that
cannot track frequent operational policy updates and
exhibits systemic hallucinations [3], [4]. While continual
fine-tuning has been explored to ingest proprietary en-
terprise guidelines, it suffers from catastrophic forgetting,
high computational expense, and a total lack of factual
auditability. Non-parametric augmentation provides an
external memory mechanism without modifying neural
weights. Our framework extends non-parametric ground-
ing by incorporating executable process models alongside
textual SOPs.
B. Retrieval-Augmented Generation Paradigms
Retrieval-Augmented Generation decouples memoriza-
tion from factual generation by fetching relevant external
passages prior to synthesis [5], [6]. Standard implemen-
tations leverage dense dual-encoder architectures such
as DPR [7] and Sentence-BERT [8] with approximate
nearest-neighbor search [10]. While effective for open-
domain factual retrieval, dense embeddings map text into
metric spaces where distance is fundamentally symmetric:
dist(u,v ) = dist(v,u ) [9]. In procedural enterprise tasks,
this symmetry induces topological scrambling, rendering
vector retrieval blind to chronological causality. Our
framework resolves this by coupling vector retrieval with
directed graph traversal.
C. Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GraphRAG)
GraphRAG integrates structured knowledge graphs into
retrieval pipelines to overcome text chunk fragmentation
[11]–[14]. While constructing entity-relation graphs im-
proves multi-hop reasoning over associative facts, standard
knowledge graphs represent static entity triples (h,r,t )
rather than dynamic operational workflows. They lack
explicit execution semantics, such as directed sequence
flows, state transitions, organizational swimlanes, and con-
ditional decision gateways [17]. In contrast, our approach
extracts attributed directed multigraphs directly from
standardized BPMN 2.0 XML specifications.
D. Business Process Intelligence and BPMN Formalisms
Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN 2.0,
ISO/IEC 19510) provides the international standard for
business process modeling [17], [18], [20]. Business Process
Management (BPM) research has developed sophisticated
process mining and compliance verification techniques [18],
[19]. Recent studies have explored NLP interfaces for pro-
cess models, including natural language process generation
and automated text-to-model alignment [25]. However,
these approaches focus on process discovery or model
translation rather than integrating process models into
retrieval-augmented generative architectures for enterprise
question answering.
E. Agentic RAG and Self-Reflective Architectures
Agentic RAG architectures introduce autonomous plan-
ning and iterative self-reflection to dynamically optimize
retrieval-generation cycles [21]–[24]. Frameworks such as
Self-RAG [21] and CRAG [23] incorporate reflection to-
kens and dynamic tool dispatch. However, existing agentic
systems remain confined to selecting external search tools
over homogeneous textual collections; they lack adaptive
routing mechanisms capable of dynamically partitioning
queries between text vector stores and directed process
graphs.
F. Research Positioning
Table I positions the proposed framework against repre-
sentative prior paradigms. While prior approaches address
isolated dimensions, our framework provides an integrated
architecture uniting dual-modal representation, execution
semantics, adaptive routing, and closed-loop verification.

B. SOP Vector Representation
The textual SOP knowledge space Ksop comprises n
unstructured or semi-structured policy documents Ksop =
{c1,c 2,...,c n}. To preserve hierarchical document prove-
nance, each chunk ci encapsulates Abstract Syntax Tree
(AST) section headers and metadata:
ci =⟨Path(H1 >H 2 >H 3), Bodyi, Metai⟩ (3)
Chunk ci is mapped into a dense continuous embedding
eci ∈ Rd via encoder Membed (Google text-embedding-
004, d = 768 ) and indexed in a persistent HNSW cosine
index in ChromaDB. While Ksop provides continuous
semantic coverage over declarative clauses, attachments,
and fee tiers, it lacks temporal directionality due to vector
symmetry (dist (u,v ) = dist(v,u )).
C. BPMN Graph Formalism and Transformation Pipeline
To enforce non-symmetric chronological precedence and
conditional logic, the process knowledge space Kbpmn is
structured as an attributed directed multigraph:
G = (V,E,L,S,C) (4)
where:
• V =Vtask∪Vevent∪Vgateway is the set of operational
nodes.
• E⊆V×V represents directed sequence flow transi-
tions (e = (u,v ) =⇒ dist(u,v )̸= dist(v,u )).
• L : V → Σ∗ assigns canonical natural language
activity labels.
• S : V → Ractor assigns authorized swimlane roles
(e.g., Author, Treasurer).
• C : Egateway→ Φ maps exclusive decision gateway
edges to boolean guards (e.g., [Valid] vs. [Invalid]).

For exclusive decision gateways vg ∈ Vgateway, outgoing
sequence edges e ∈ Out(vg) are evaluated against con-
dition guards C(e), isolating opposing conditional paths
(e.g., [Approved] vs. [Rejected]) and eliminating gateway
branch ambiguity.
D. Query Understanding Agent (QUA)
User inquiries in enterprise environments frequently
contain informal vernacular and institutional acronyms.
The Query Understanding Agent normalizes incoming
query q. It expands acronyms via institutional dictionary
Dacronym and performs fuzzy/semantic grounding against
canonical BPMN activity labels L(V ) to yield normalized
query q′ (Algorithm 1).

B. Evaluation Design Rationale
To ensure methodological transparency and defensibility
for Q1 journal review, the experimental design addresses
four critical evaluation considerations:
1) Rationale for Stratified Comparative Benchmark
Sample ( N = 20 ): Evaluating multi-agent closed-

overhead. Unlike single-pass RAG pipelines, our
architecture coordinates multi-stage agentic LLM
executions: query rewriting, dynamic quota rout-

generation, adversarial verification across four audit
modules, and conditional self-repair regeneration.
Evaluating five competing architectures across this
closed-loop pipeline alongside multi-aspect RAGAS
evaluator LLM judges generates over 300 remote
LLM invocations per benchmark run. Selecting a
balanced stratified test sample of 20 queries (exactly
5 per category) prevents remote API rate-limit
exhaustion (e.g., Google GenAI HTTP 429 burst
restrictions) while maintaining statistical power.
2) Role of the Complete Corpus ( N = 338): The entire
N = 338 corpus is deployed for comprehensive
evaluation of the Adaptive Router Agent, measur-
ing intent distribution stability, token compression
eﬀiciency (−6.5%), and dispatch accuracy across all
operational workflows (Section V-D).

VI. Discussion
A. Resolving Inductive Limitations of Vector RAG and
GraphRAG
The empirical findings demonstrate that standard Vec-
tor RAG and GraphRAG fail in enterprise procedural QA
due to fundamental structural assumptions:
1) Metric Distance Symmetry vs. Directional Causality:
Vector RAG relies on metric spaces where distance is
symmetric (dist (u,v ) = dist(v,u )). Our findings es-
tablish that mapping procedural documents into un-
ordered vector spaces inevitably induces topological
scrambling. Attributed directed multigraphs enforce
non-symmetric transition validity ( e = (u,v ) = ⇒
dist(u,v )̸= dist(v,u )).
2) Static Entity Relational Triples vs. Dynamic Exe-
cution Semantics: Existing GraphRAG frameworks
extract static relational triples (h,r,t ). However,
enterprise governance requires condition guards, exe-
cution states, and swimlane segregations. Attributed
BPMN graphs explicitly model exclusive gateways
and swimlane boundaries.

D. Human Evaluation Limitation and Future Validation
Roadmap
A recognized limitation of this study is its reliance
on automated evaluation frameworks (NLI entailment,
exact graph reachability via NetworkX DFS, and regex-
grounded swimlane attribution). While automated metrics
guarantee deterministic reproducibility and eliminate hu-
man subject bias, they cannot fully capture real-world user
trust or operational edge cases.
To address this limitation, future work will conduct an
empirical human evaluation campaign:
• Expert Practitioner Panel: A panel of 10 certified
enterprise process practitioners, conference general
chairs, and institutional treasurers.
• Double-Blind Evaluation Protocol: 50 challenging
enterprise inquiries evaluated across three qualitative
dimensions: (1) Operational Actionability, (2) Attri-
bution Clarity, and (3) Audit Defensibility on a 5-
point Likert scale.
VII. Conclusion
This paper introduced an Adaptive Agentic Hybrid
RAG framework with BPMN-grounded graph reasoning
to address the heterogeneous enterprise knowledge routing
problem. By formalizing enterprise knowledge across un-
structured SOP vector collections and structured BPMN
2.0 attributed directed graphs, the framework substan-
tially mitigates the inductive limitations of standard
vector RAG (topological scrambling and gateway branch
ambiguity) and GraphRAG (absence of execution seman-
tics and swimlanes). Empirical evaluation on the ICICoS
ProcessQA benchmark test suite demonstrates that the
proposed architecture achieves a Faithfulness of 0.972,
reduces hallucinations to 2.6% (a 76.4% relative error
reduction vs Traditional RAG and 79.5% vs Hybrid RAG),
and attains 65.0% Router Accuracy and 93.8% Actor
Attribution Accuracy while achieving 1.000 Context Recall
on the evaluated benchmark sample ( p< 0.001, Wilcoxon
signed-rankp = 0.000219). Rigorous statistical testing and
component ablations confirm that deterministic workflow
grounding combined with closed-loop agentic auditing
provides an effective, scientifically defensible foundation
